Effective dashboard and monitoring systems are essential for supervising robotic operations, particularly in search and rescue scenarios where real-time situational awareness is critical. The literature emphasizes that operators must be able to observe robot status, sensor data, and perception outputs in a clear and responsive manner to support informed decision-making during missions.

Web-based dashboards are widely adopted in robotic systems due to their cross-platform accessibility and ease of use. By leveraging modern web technologies, monitoring interfaces can be accessed through standard devices without the need for specialized software. However, web-based approaches introduce limitations in rendering performance, native OS integration, and real-time responsiveness that can be critical for robotics supervision. After evaluating both web-based and native desktop frameworks during development, the project adopted PySide6, a Python binding for the Qt framework, to build a native desktop operations center. This decision was driven by PySide6’s superior rendering performance for real-time map visualization, its support for dockable panels and rich widget layouts, and its ability to integrate directly with ZeroMQ transport and to host YOLO inference inside the desktop application. Rather than running the model in the UI thread, the console isolates YOLO in a dedicated crash-isolated subprocess, so a model crash, hang, or out-of-memory event cannot freeze or kill the operator console — a level of native integration and process control that a browser-based dashboard cannot provide.

Integration between robotic systems and web-based dashboards is commonly achieved through ROS-compatible communication frameworks. ROS web bridge technologies enable real-time data exchange between ROS nodes and external interfaces, allowing monitoring applications to receive live updates from the robot. Communication protocols such as WebSockets are frequently used to support continuous, low-latency data transmission, which is necessary for displaying time-sensitive information such as robot status, sensor readings, and perception results. In this project, rosbridge is available as an optional debug-only fallback path but is not used in the production monitoring workflow; the primary data path uses ZMQ/msgpack channels connected directly to the PySide6 dashboard.

Map and environment visualization are also key components of effective monitoring systems. The literature highlights the importance of presenting SLAM outputs and spatial information in an intuitive format that allows operators to understand both the robot’s surroundings and its position within the environment. Proper visualization of occupancy grid maps, combined with perception and system state information, enhances situational awareness and supports safer and more efficient operation.

Overall, dashboard and monitoring frameworks play a critical role in bridging the gap between autonomous robotic systems and human operators. By enabling real-time observation, interaction, and visualization, these systems support reliable supervision and contribute significantly to the effectiveness of robotic platforms in complex and dynamic environments.

Before selecting and purchasing hardware components, we conducted a comprehensive review of commonly used platforms and devices in mobile robotic systems, as illustrated in Figure 2.1. The aim of this review was to identify hardware solutions that are reliable, well-supported, and suitable for the computational and operational requirements of the proposed system.

Processing platforms play a central role in managing perception, communication, and control tasks in robotic systems. Single-board computers and microcontrollers are often used together to balance computational capability and real-time performance.

The Raspberry Pi was reviewed as a primary onboard computing platform due to its widespread use in robotics. The Raspberry Pi 4, particularly the 8 GB variant, offers sufficient processing power and memory to support ROS-based applications, including SLAM and high-level system coordination. The Raspberry Pi 3B+, while less powerful, was also considered suitable for secondary or supporting roles. Strong community support, extensive documentation, and the availability of general-purpose input/output (GPIO) pins make the Raspberry Pi a practical choice for integrating sensors and peripheral devices.

In addition to single-board computers, Arduino microcontrollers are commonly employed for low-level, real-time control tasks. The Arduino Mega was reviewed due to its large number of input/output pins, which allows it to interface with multiple sensors and motors simultaneously. Arduino platforms are particularly well suited for handling timing-critical operations such as motor control, and they benefit from a wide range of well-documented libraries that simplify development and integration.

A variety of sensors are required to support perception, navigation, and obstacle avoidance in mobile robotic systems. The literature review focused on sensors that are widely adopted and compatible with ROS-based architectures.

Two-dimensional LiDAR sensors are commonly used for SLAM and environment mapping. The review considered factors such as sensing range, measurement accuracy, scan frequency, and ROS compatibility. These sensors provide reliable spatial information that is essential for building occupancy grid maps and supporting navigation tasks.

Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) were reviewed for their role in providing orientation and motion data. Considerations included the number of sensing axes (such as 6-axis or 9-axis configurations), measurement accuracy, and ease of integration with other sensors. IMU data is particularly useful for improving localization performance when combined with odometry information.

Ultrasonic sensors were also reviewed as a low-cost solution for short-range obstacle detection. While they do not provide the same level of detail as LiDAR, ultrasonic sensors are effective for detecting nearby objects and can complement other sensors in close-range scenarios.

For visual perception, USB cameras were reviewed for use in object and human detection tasks. Key evaluation criteria included image resolution, frame rate, and compatibility with common image processing and deep learning libraries. Cameras provide essential visual information that supports high-level perception and interaction with the environment.

The drive system is a critical component that directly affects robot stability and control. The literature review examined various motor and drivetrain configurations commonly used in mobile robots.

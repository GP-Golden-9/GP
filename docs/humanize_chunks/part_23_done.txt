Dashboard Integration: The PySide6 Operations Center successfully integrated a number of asynchronous streams (ZMQ map/telemetry/video/health and ESP32 HTTP telemetry). The interface used QSettings to keep a clean layout persistent and enabled global A* route planning in inflated occupancy grids without blocking the UI thread.

Even though the results were successful, some limitations were found during the empirical testing. While these limitations do not invalidate the system design, they point to the main technical areas that require further optimisation before the platform can be scaled up to larger or more demanding environments.

Odometry and Wheel Slip Beta's localisation is based on dead reckoning -- a complementary fusion of the 25GA370 wheel encoders and GY-87 gyro Z-rate, calculated laptop-side. The gyro fusion and per-unit scale calibration helped with reducing the rotational drift, however translational wheel slip still accumulates error over long explorations. This means that frequent updates of set-poses on the dashboard are needed to align Beta with Alpha’s global map frame. Gamma is not a ROS robot and doesn't run any odometry stack from Beta. It's a non-ROS ESP32 inspector exposing an HTTP/JSON telemetry endpoint. Reported pose is ESP32's own coarse estimate, not the encoder/IMU complementary filter used on Beta.

Network Dependencies: Video stream transmission over port 5560 depends on the strength of the WiFi signal. Packet dropouts happened in corners with high WiFi attenuation which were mitigated by the ZMQ gateway connection retries and ESP32 connection self-healing loops .

Beta Ultrasonics Beta has two front-facing HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensors wired and active in the current build (LEFT TRIG=30/ECHO=31, RIGHT TRIG=32/ECHO=33, 5 V) mounted on the front edge and tilted slightly outward for field of view. The firmware median-filters these and appends the ranges to the serial packet. The bridge publishes /ultrasonic/{left,right}. It hard-stops forward motion under 25 cm (re-enabling above 40 cm with hysteresis). The goto controller starts a proportional slow-down under 60 cm. That’s the only real-time obstacle sense Beta has, because it doesn’t have lidar. Other limitations: (1) GY-87 IMU intermittent I2C dropout -- on a marginal power line Beta's GY-87 intermittently drops off the I2C bus, after which the firmware streams frozen non-zero gyro values, heading freezes, and the map arrow goes static until a power-cycle. A fallback with encoder-heading keeps driven turns working. Pending fix: rewire VCC 3.3 V to 5 V and resolder. (2) Gamma over-voltage damage -- a buck regulator (set to 10V) was attached to Gamma's ESP32, IMU & MQ sensor, ESP32 survived, but the onboard IMU is probably dead (the firmware can deal with its absence) and the MQ sensor needs to be re-verified. (3) Poor performance of fire detection model -- fire.pt only detects real fire with a confidence of 28-37%, which is far below the 0.80 alarm gate, so a better trained model is required for reliable fire alarming. (4) Residual yaw drift - after gyro scale calibration, a multi-turn run has about 25 deg of rotational drift remaining. (5) LAN only, no GPS -- the system is implemented over a dedicated offline LAN with no internet and no GPS, so all localisation is relative and absolute positioning is not available. (6) No 30-minute real-robot soak yet -- network KPIs only validated against in-process simulator over loopback; no full 30-minute real-robot field soak documented.

These constraints demonstrate that the current prototype is effective for controlled indoor exploration, but future work should concentrate on more robust localisation, stronger network planning and better multi-robot map synchronisation.

The system was able to autonomously map indoors, track through custom complementary filtered odometry, detect visual hazards, and continuously monitor the environment by implementing a cooperative three-robot fleet (Alpha, Beta, Gamma).

The project demonstrated the feasibility of offloading computationally heavy tasks, namely the YOLO fire and smoke detection pipeline (defaulting to fire.pt) running in a crash-isolated subprocess, to the central Emergency Operations Center running a native PySide6 desktop dashboard, while maintaining real-time, slam_toolbox mapping and custom complementary filtered control onboard the robots through effective integration of hardware and software components.

The project achieved some important milestones laid out in the initial planning phase, demonstrating the viability of the architecture deployed:

Hardware and Mechanical Design: The team designed and built a stable four-wheel differential-drive mobile base.

Power Stability: Systematic diagnosis and mitigation of power-delivery issues. The platform has a 3S LiPo with a variable XL4015 buck converter to protect sensitive electronics like the Raspberry Pi from voltage brownouts from the motors. Stable delivery was an on-going engineering problem, not a one-time design. Brownout crash-loops were traced to a 0.4-0.5 V drop along the cable-and-connector path between the buck terminals and the Pi pins, and was corrected by tuning the buck against a reading taken at the Pi pins under load (with bulk capacitors added at the buck output and the Pi connector). The team also takes note, as a hard-learned lesson, of an over-voltage event where a buck set to 10 V damaged peripherals on the inspector robot (Gamma); the procedure now adopted is to measure voltage at the terminals before each connection.

Autonomous Mapping and Localisation: The system has successfully generated two-dimensional occupancy grid maps with a resolution of 0.025 m/cell using the slam_toolbox algorithm and an RPLidar A1M8 sensor.

Sensor Fusion : Implemented a custom complementary filter (fusing 25GA370 wheel encoders with the GY-87 IMU gyroscope Z-axis rate) that significantly improved rotational stability and reduced drift, with auto-bias calibration and a per-unit gyro scale correction protecting heading tracking. Two limitations are admitted frankly. First, there is about 25 degrees of residual yaw drift after calibration (down from roughly 45 degrees before) due to the IMU’s per-unit scale tolerance, which is why the platform is better described as dead-reckoning rather than drift-free localisation. Second, the Beta's GY-87 IMU sporadically drops off the I2C bus, at which point the firmware streams frozen gyro values and the heading freezes until a power cycle. The top open hardware ticket is a hardware rewire of the IMU supply (from 3.3 V to 5 V) and resolder. A heading fallback to encoders only to survive a total gyro dropout.

Visual Perception : We implemented a real-time hazard-detection pipeline using a primary clean COCO YOLOv8s network (person/dog/cat) in combination with a secondary fire-only neural model (fire.pt) and a classical flame-property detector for saturated red-and-yellow flame shapes. Neural fire model: We only trust the single “Fire” class (does not reliably give separate smoke or flame classes). It is important to be honest and say that the neural fire model has limited confidence on real fire imagery (in the order of 28-37 percent against a 0.80 alarm gate) so reliable demonstration detection is currently based on the classical flame-property detector. The primary future work for this project is the improvement of the accuracy of the neural fire model on real fire (see Section 6.3).

Offloaded Inference: deep learning inference was offloaded to a child process via a ZeroMQ video stream, which allowed smooth visualisation at the desired video KPI of >=12 FPS without impacting the robots’ onboard control and performance.

Environmental Sensing : An MQ-5 gas sensor was integrated on the ESP32 Gamma platform, with firmware that triggers latched local alarms and dashboard warning banners when readings exceed the configured ADC-count threshold. One caveat must be entered. Gamma's electronics were hit by an over-voltage incident (a buck set incorrectly to 10V). The ESP32 itself seems fine and has been verified, but the onboard IMU is likely damaged and the reading and alarm threshold of the MQ gas sensor should be re-verified before trusting gas detection. The firmware is written to tolerate a missing IMU.

Real-Time Monitoring: Built a native PySide6 desktop dashboard for centralised monitoring, parsing and aggregating data from multiple ZeroMQ and HTTP streams to display the SLAM map, YOLO detection results, and real-time gas telemetry with customisable dock layouts.

Although the current prototype serves well as an integrated cyber-physical platform, there are a number of improvements needed to scale the system and increase its robustness in more demanding environments:

Battery Monitoring Integration: Move from existing chassis mounted LED voltmeters to fully integrated firmware based ADC battery monitoring. This includes completing the wiring of the hardware voltage divider circuit to the analogue pins and updating the firmware of the micro-controller to publish live logic and actuator battery cell levels directly to the operations dashboard.

Sensor Fusion and Odometry Improvements: Upgraded to advanced visual odometry or back to an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) sensor fusion framework to minimise dead-reckoning drift. Additional perception markers or secondary wheel encoders can be employed to reduce further accumulated translational slip on low-friction tile surfaces.

Video Stream Protocol Optimisation: Optimise the video streaming protocols to overcome high WiFi attenuation on port 5560. This includes things like adaptive bitrate streaming, hardware accelerated H.264/H.265 compression, or fallback low resolution modes to keep the stream fluid when robots are driving behind concrete walls.

Better Fire-Detection Model: Training a fire-detection model on the team’s own footage so that real fire scores much higher than the look-alike false positives that currently overlap it. This is the main recognised limitation of the project (Section 6.2): given the current neural fire model, a usable alarm threshold cannot distinguish real fire from false positives, so the real fix is to train a better model, not to raise the threshold. IMU hardware fix Move the Beta's GY-87 IMU power input from the 3.3V pin to 5V and re-solder the connections, to get rid of the intermittent I2C bus dropouts that currently freeze the heading estimate. Then redo the gyro scale calibration to reduce residual yaw drift. Direct Robot-to-Robot Communication (optional): The fleet is centralised by design and all inter-robot coordination is brokered through the operator console. A future enhancement could add direct robot-to-robot messaging for tighter cooperative behaviours without a console in the loop.

The demonstrated capabilities of the Swarm Robot System makes it highly suitable for real world applications where human access is limited, risky or inefficient.

Indoor Facility Monitoring and Inspection (main target): The system can create occupancy maps, identify visual hazards, and sample for hazardous gases in controlled indoor environments such as warehouses, industrial floors, hospitals, and similar facilities without endangering personnel. This is the deployment class the current prototype is indeed tuned for: it runs on a self-contained LAN with no internet and no GPS, it navigates by dead-reckoning (with the approximate 25-degree residual yaw drift noted in Section 6.2), and it is calibrated for flat indoor floors. Search and Rescue (long-term aspiration): Search and rescue in collapsed buildings and disaster zones is an exciting long-term goal, but to achieve it will require significant additional effort beyond the current prototype (e.g. robust localisation on unstructured rubble, communication independent of a fixed LAN, and ruggedised mobility). This is proposed here as an avenue for future research, not a demonstrated capability.

Industrial and Facility Monitoring The autonomous navigation, environment mapping and gas sensing features can be directly deployed in large operational environments such as industrial companies, warehouses and hospitals.

Routine inspections: Robots can conduct routine inspections, safety checks and ongoing environmental monitoring activities without disrupting daily operations or putting personnel at risk.
